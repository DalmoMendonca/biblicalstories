# 104 Sacred Histories

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform
web

## Stack
Static-first HTML, CSS, and ES modules with Python archival import. Build to `dist` for Netlify. No framework/runtime API dependencies in the published site.

## Users
Readers and scholars interested in Hübner's *104 Biblical Histories*, its historical engravings, its use in biblical education, and the Russian childhood reading world remembered by Dostoevsky.

## Product Purpose
Make the historic 104-story book explorable through its original imagery, with accessible source folios, original-language provenance, and multilingual navigation.

## Positioning
The picture-book is the interface. Every chapter is anchored to its **actual historical engraving**, instead of stock imagery, modern illustrations, or a conventional text-first Bible reader.

## Operating Context
Mobile-first visual exploration with desktop deep reading. The 1768 Giessen German printing contains 104 captioned engraved plates (52 per testament), preserved and digitized by Heidelberg University Library. Other editions have different print histories and must not be conflated.

## Capabilities and Constraints
- Browse all 104 engraved stories and switch site/navigation language between English, German, and Russian.
- Zoom historical artwork; navigate between stories, search names, filter Testaments, save stories locally.
- Read historical German original folios via primary facsimiles and access Zürich's uncorrrected German OCR.
- Translation interface labels and story titles are **modern editorial navigation labels** (not transcriptions of historical title lines).
- Verified eighteenth-century Russian and English story texts are not yet available in accurately segmented, checked digital form. They must not be fabricated or mislabeled.
- The full 1815 Russian 35+1 image sequence is not yet obtained. German 1768 plates are a separate historical source, never represented as Dostoevsky's exact illustration set.

## Brand Commitments
A beautiful image-driven edition emphasizing the actual eighteenth-century plates; original source integrity is more important than a visually complete but fictitious transcription.

## Evidence on Hand
- Heidelberg 1768 IIIF manifest, 567 folios and exactly 104 labeled original engraved plates, downloaded individually and retained as archival-derived WebP images.
- Zürich c.1775 IIIF manifest (632 canvases) and whole-book uncorrected OCR.
- 1765 Hamburg German/French/English parallel publication identified but no verified per-story transcription.
- 1815 Russian translation by Vasily Bogorodsky identified, historically associated with Dostoevsky, 35 plus frontispiece documented but scans unavailable.

## Product Principles
1. The actual engraving leads; the interface recedes.
2. Cite each picture and text to its specific bibliographic witness.
3. Never substitute OCR or AI writing for a historical original without clear disclosure.
4. Image and full-book navigation work on phones.
5. Public data and portable open formats; no user account required.
